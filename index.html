      Variables

      VariableDescription

      Earlier update files named the narrow indices c_base and c_fiscal; these are now c_nar and c_fisc, following the paper. The values are unchanged. Every number on this site is reproduced by the build pipeline, which checks itself against the published paper and earlier releases.

      Method

      State capacity is the ability of a government to achieve the goals it sets: raise revenue efficiently, control its territory, enforce the law impartially, and provide public goods that support markets. The index is the first principal component of six indicators from the Varieties of Democracy (V-Dem) dataset, each covering one of these dimensions.

      Factor loadings from the principal component analysis on V-Dem v9 (Table 2 of the paper). Inputs are standardised before weighting, so a loading is the weight of a one-standard-deviation change in that input.

      Three versions

      • Narrow (c_nar) uses rule of law, state authority over territory, impartial public administration and public goods spending. It has the longest coverage, back to 1789 for more than 20 countries.
      • Narrow with fiscal capacity (c_fisc) adds the state’s main source of revenue. Fiscal data are missing for some important cases, for example the United States between 1920 and 2005.
      • Comprehensive (c_comp) adds educational equality. It covers every dimension but starts in 1900.

      Updates to newer V-Dem releases

      The original series is the published one, built on V-Dem v9 and ending in 2018. Each update applies the same fixed v9 loadings to a newer V-Dem release. The inputs are standardised on that release’s own sample, so each vintage has mean zero. Because V-Dem re-estimates its whole history with each release, an update revises earlier years slightly as well as adding new ones. Correlations with the original series on overlapping years are about 0.99. The download files also include pca_* columns that re-estimate the principal component on the newer data, for robustness checks.

        Alternative assumptions

        The control bar can switch on assumptions that fill gaps in the V-Dem data. Polities scored under an assumption are labelled “(assumed)” in every view. They are scored with the same loadings and standardisation constants as everyone else, so no other country’s score changes, although ranks below them shift. The official download files never include them; each assumption has its own file on the Download page.

          Summary statistics. Scores are principal-component scores and are not rescaled, so the standard deviation equals the square root of the first eigenvalue. The original rows reproduce Table 3 of the paper.
